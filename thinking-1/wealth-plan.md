## 9. Phase 6 — What to Avoid (Munger's Inversion)

> "All I want to know is where I'm going to die, so I'll never go there." — Charlie Munger.
> Avoiding these mistakes matters more than picking winners. Review this list every quarter.

### 9.1 The biggest financial mistakes to avoid
- Staying in low-priced local work when the same skill sells for 3-5x abroad — silent opportunity cost.
- Carrying high-interest / "5-6" debt while trying to invest at the same time.
- Lifestyle inflation: raising spending every time income rises, so the savings rate never improves.
- No emergency fund -> forced to sell assets or borrow at the worst possible moment.
- One income source, or one client above ~40% of revenue.
- Not tracking the numbers — flying blind on cash flow, receivables, taxes, runway.
- Ignoring BIR taxes until penalties and surcharges compound.
- Co-signing loans, or lending money you can't afford to lose (especially to relatives).
- Get-rich-quick bets: leveraged crypto, forex, options, "double your money" schemes, MLM, online gambling / e-sabong.
- Buying depreciating liabilities on installment (car, newest phone, gadgets) and calling them "assets".
- Waiting for the "perfect time" to start investing — years of compounding lost to hesitation.
- Selling investments in a panic during a crash (locking in the loss).
- Paying for expensive "guru" / mentorship programs instead of doing the reps.
- Keeping all money in one bank, one currency, one country.
- No health insurance -> one hospitalization erases years of savings.
- No will / named beneficiaries once assets exist.

### 9.2 Common pitfalls of the middle class
- Trading time for money forever, with no equity or ownership stake in anything.
- Believing a bigger salary equals wealth — it's the savings rate and the assets that decide it.
- "Keeping up": spending to signal status (car, brands, gadgets, oversized debut / wedding / birthdays).
- Buying a house too big, too early, too leveraged — and calling the mortgage an investment.
- Treating 13th-month pay, bonuses and tax refunds as "free money" to spend.
- Financing lifestyle with revolving credit-card balances and "buy now, pay later".
- Diversifying too early — spreading thin before the main income engine exists.
- Depending on one employer or one platform for 100% of income and identity.
- Permanently subsidising relatives' consumption instead of one-time help or a shared investment.
- Saving in a regular bank account at ~0.1% while inflation quietly erodes it.
- Buying VUL / insurance-investment products sold as "forced savings".
- No estate planning — assets frozen in probate for years.
- "I'll invest when I earn more" — then never actually starting.
- Confusing being busy with being productive, and productive with being wealthy.

### 9.3 Bad investments (for your situation, right now)
- Leveraged crypto, meme coins, token "presales".
- Forex, CFDs, binary options, day-trading.
- Whole-life / VUL / endowment insurance — high fees, weak returns. Buy term + invest the difference.
- Pre-selling condos as "investment" — oversupply, ~3-4% gross yields, high fees, developer risk.
- Time-share and "agri" / memorial-lot / farm-lot schemes promising guaranteed returns.
- MLM / network-marketing "business opportunities".
- Friends' or relatives' startups with no term sheet, no equity documents, no governance.
- Individual stock picking on tips, with no research and no diversification.
- Actively managed funds charging 2%+ per year versus a low-cost index.
- Expensive gear / courses framed as "investing in myself" beyond what the current work needs.
- Informal lending "with interest" to acquaintances — principal rarely comes back.
- Gold bars, collectibles, NFTs as a core holding.
- A car — unless it directly and measurably raises your income.

### 9.4 Invisible waste (audit quarterly — these leak six figures per decade)
- Forgotten subscriptions: SaaS tools, streaming, cloud storage, gym, trials that auto-converted.
- FX and remittance spread on every international payout — use low-fee rails (Wise), batch withdrawals.
- Platform and processor fees (Upwork ~10%, PayPal FX spread) — move trusted clients to direct contracts.
- Creep in "small" recurring things: daily coffee / delivery, ride-hailing vs commute, convenience-store runs.
- Interest and late fees on cards, loans, and utility bills.
- Tax overpaid or penalised — not electing the 8% option, missing allowable deductions, filing late.
- Idle cash at ~0% in a traditional savings account instead of 4-6% digital bank / MP2.
- Buying tools and gadgets for a "someday" project.
- Food spoilage and unplanned grocery trips.
- Extended warranties, unneeded insurance riders, mobile data / "load" plans bigger than your usage.
- Time waste: doing PHP 300/hr admin you could automate or delegate; unpaid scope creep on projects; doomscrolling.
- Not invoicing promptly or not chasing receivables — money earned but never collected.
- Currency drag: holding all savings in PHP while earning USD and the peso depreciates — keep some USD.

### 9.5 Limiting beliefs about money (replace each one)
- "Money is the root of evil / rich people are greedy." -> Money is a tool and a scoreboard for value delivered; you can be generous *and* wealthy.
- "I'm just a developer, not a business person." -> Every freelancer is a business of one; pricing and sales are learnable skills.
- "You need money to make money." -> You need a valuable skill + distribution; capital follows.
- "Charging more is greedy / clients here can't pay." -> Then change who you sell to; price to the client's ROI, not to your comfort.
- "Investing is gambling / it's only for the rich." -> Broad index investing is the opposite of gambling — it's owning the whole economy.
- "I'll start investing when I earn more." -> Habit and time in the market beat the amount; start with PHP 1,000.
- "It's too late for me at 34." -> You have 25-30+ compounding years; most wealth is built after 40.
- "Rich people just got lucky or had connections." -> Luck helps once; skill + reps + patience + not blowing up is the repeatable part.
- "If I focus on money I'll lose my creativity / soul." -> Financial stability funds better work and removes desperation.
- "Everyone has debt, it's normal." -> Consumer debt is a wealth transfer from you to lenders; "normal" isn't the target.
- "More income will fix everything." -> Only if savings rate and asset-building rise with it — otherwise the hole just gets bigger.
- "I have to say yes to every family financial request." -> A set giving budget + boundaries protect the goose; you help more by building wealth first.

**Munger's summary:** "It is remarkable how much long-term advantage people like us have gotten by
trying to be consistently not stupid, instead of trying to be very intelligent."

## 10. Phase 7 — How the Wealthy Think

> Mindset is not magic — it is a set of repeatable mental habits. Each contrast below is followed by
> the specific translation for a 34-year-old freelance developer in the Philippines.

### 10.1 How the rich think differently
- **Balance sheet, not paycheck.** Broke thinking optimises this month's income; wealthy thinking asks
  "what do I *own* that pays me?" -> Give every peso of profit a job: buy an asset or build one.
- **Ownership over wages.** They want equity, royalties, and businesses — not only billable hours.
  -> Convert client work into retainers, then products, then a company.
- **Leverage (Naval's four): labour, capital, code, media.** Code and media are permissionless and
  compound. -> Your code + a small niche audience can serve thousands at near-zero marginal cost.
- **Decades, not paydays.** They let compounding do the heavy lifting and judge choices by their
  10-year effect. -> "Where does this put me in 2036?" beats "can I afford this now?"
- **Positive-sum.** Wealth is created, not just divided; making others win creates your win.
  -> Price for the value you create, not the hours you endure.
- **Money is a tool, not a status signal.** They buy assets; the middle class buys the *appearance*
  of wealth. -> Keep the old laptop and phone; buy the index fund.
- **Spend on time and health; economise on ego.** Pay to remove drudgery and protect your body;
  never pay to impress. -> Outsource low-value admin early; keep lifestyle flat as income rises.
- **Identity = owner / builder / investor**, not "an employee who is careful with money."

### 10.2 How they see risk
- **The real risk is having no assets and one income stream** — not market volatility. Staying
  uninvested *is* the risky position over 20 years.
- **Volatility is not loss.** A diversified index falling 30% is noise; leverage, or one concentrated
  coin, is how you actually reach zero. Buffett's "never lose money" means *avoid ruin*, not avoid fluctuation.
- **Survive first (Dalio).** Never take a risk that can knock you out of the game. Keep reserves,
  avoid leverage, insure catastrophes (health, liability).
- **Seek asymmetry.** Bets with small capped downside and large upside (a product launch, a
  discount-for-equity deal). Avoid the reverse — big or unbounded downside for a small gain
  (informal lending, selling options, co-signing).
- **Risk is priced, not simply avoided.** The question is "am I paid enough for *this specific* risk,
  and can I survive the worst realistic case?"
- **Diversify what can ruin you; concentrate what can build you.** One business focus, a diversified
  portfolio underneath it.
- **Reversible vs. irreversible.** Take reversible risks fast and often; slow down only for the few
  one-way doors (large debt, co-signing, selling the business).

### 10.3 How they see opportunities
- **An opportunity is an unsolved problem someone will pay to remove** — they hunt pain, not "ideas."
- **Specific knowledge + an unfair advantage.** They pursue what they're uniquely positioned to do.
  -> Yours: web performance + AI tooling + a PH cost base + fluent English. Few competitors have all four.
- **Distribution beats product.** An email list, a client roster, a marketplace ranking, an audience —
  owning a channel is where money compounds.
- **Boring and profitable > exciting and speculative.** Care plans, B2B templates, a niche agency:
  unglamorous, durable cash flow.
- **They say no to almost everything (Buffett's 20-slot punch card).** A few great bets beat many
  mediocre ones; most "opportunities" are distractions.
- **Time-arbitrage.** Being early to a shift — right now, AI-assisted delivery and AI micro-tools —
  lets a small player win before incumbents move.
- **They buy in downturns.** Fear puts good assets on sale; they keep dry powder for it.
- **Compounding relationships.** Play long-term games with long-term people; one trusted client or
  partner can generate a decade of deals.

### 10.4 How they make decisions
- **Invert first (Munger).** "What would guarantee failure here?" — then avoid that before optimising
  for success.
- **Expected value, not certainty.** Weigh payoff x probability, and always ask "worst realistic
  case — can I take it?"
- **Second-order thinking (Dalio).** "And then what?" Trace consequences two or three steps out.
- **Opportunity cost is the true cost.** Every yes to a cheap client is a no to prospecting a premium one.
- **Circle of competence.** Big bets only where you truly understand the mechanics. "Too hard" is a
  valid answer.
- **Match speed to reversibility.** Fast on cheap, reversible calls (which niche to test, which tool);
  slow, written analysis on expensive, irreversible ones (debt, hiring, a sale).
- **Data + reflection loop.** Track outcomes, review weekly, update the approach. Pain + Reflection = Progress.
- **Detach ego from being right.** Change your mind quickly when facts change; the goal is the correct
  answer, not the defended one.
- **Pre-commit and automate.** Remove willpower from the loop — auto-transfers, hard rules
  ("no client > 40%", "no leverage", "invest on the 5th").
- **Margin of safety.** Build room for error into every estimate — timelines, runway, expected returns.

### 10.5 How they build wealth
- **The sequence:** raise income -> widen the gap between income and lifestyle -> convert the gap into
  assets -> let assets + a business compound -> buy back your time -> repeat at a larger scale.
- **Savings rate is the engine early; returns take over later.** 50-60% saved on a rising income beats
  chasing hot returns on a small base.
- **Keep lifestyle flat while income climbs.** The whole game is the widening gap; lifestyle creep closes it.
- **One concentrated engine + a diversified base.** Build one business you control; underneath it, a
  boring global index portfolio you never touch.
- **Reinvest cash flow.** Profit buys more assets or strengthens the business — not upgrades.
  Carnegie: watch the one basket and keep pouring back in.
- **Turn skill into equity.** Productize -> systematise -> hire -> own a business that runs without you
  -> optionally sell it (2-5x profit / ARR) for a step-change in net worth.
- **Use debt only productively** — low cost, income to cover it, never for consumption.
- **Protect the downside always:** emergency fund, insurance, no single point of failure, clean
  tax/legal structure, a will.
- **Patience + consistency.** Wealth is a handful of good decisions held for a very long time, plus
  the discipline not to interrupt compounding.
- **Then give deliberately.** Once the machine runs, generosity becomes a choice you can afford —
  Carnegie's final act.

**Your one-line version:** *Sell your skill globally, keep your lifestyle small, turn the surplus into
a business you own and an index fund you never sell, and give it a decade.*
